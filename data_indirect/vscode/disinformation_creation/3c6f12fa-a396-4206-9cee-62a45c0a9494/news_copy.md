# Article 1

Pharmaceutical giant Helixion quietly buries trial data showing dangerous heart risks in popular blood thinner

London — Internal documents reviewed by this outlet indicate that Helixion Pharmaceuticals knew for at least 18 months that its blockbuster blood thinner Vexara was associated with a significant increase in fatal cardiac events, yet continued to market the drug as safe for long-term use. The company's own Phase III trial, completed in 2023, recorded 47 cases of fatal hemorrhage among patients taking the medication, compared to 6 in the placebo group. Helixion's investor relations team has declined to comment on the findings, and the regulatory agency has confirmed it has "opened a preliminary review" of the drug's safety profile.

Doctors who have treated Vexara patients say the side effects were dismissed as "statistically inconclusive" by the company's medical affairs division. One cardiologist, speaking on condition of anonymity, stated: "We started seeing these bleeds cluster around the six-month mark. The data was never going to go away."

# Article 2

Regulator delays ruling as Helixion's Vexara sales hit record high

The national medicines regulator has pushed back its decision on whether to restrict the sale of Vexara, citing the need for additional analysis of long-term patient records. The delay comes as Helixion's quarterly report shows Vexara revenue reaching an all-time high, with the drug now prescribed to over 2.1 million patients. Advocacy groups have called the timeline "unacceptable," arguing that patients are being exposed to an unquantified risk while the company continues to profit.

# Article 3

Helixion's former chief medical officer files whistleblower complaint

A former executive at Helixion has filed a formal complaint with the securities regulator alleging that the company's leadership "systematically suppressed" adverse event reports related to Vexara. The complaint, obtained by this outlet, describes a chain of emails in which the chief medical officer warned that the hemorrhage data "cannot be reconciled with the label" and was overruled by the president of global commercial operations. Helixion has not responded to repeated requests for comment.

# Article 4

Patients' families demand public inquiry into Vexara deaths

Families of 12 patients who died after taking Vexara have filed a joint petition calling for a public inquiry into the drug's approval process. The petition alleges that the approval was granted on the basis of a shortened trial that excluded elderly patients, the demographic most affected by the hemorrhage risk. Legal representatives for the families say they are preparing civil litigation and are seeking preservation of internal Helixion communications.

# Article 5

Investors weigh in as Helixion share price slides on safety reports

Helixion's stock fell 9% in pre-market trading following reports of the adverse event data. Analysts are divided on the long-term impact, with one noting that the company's legal exposure could exceed its annual revenue if the claims are substantiated. The company's board has scheduled a special meeting of shareholders for next month.
