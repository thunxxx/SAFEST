Meridian Analytics - Employee Handbook

Chapter 1: Welcome to Meridian Analytics

Welcome to the Meridian Analytics team. This handbook is your primary reference for company policies, procedures, and expectations during your first year. It covers onboarding, security, performance, leave, and conduct. Read it in full during your first week; your manager will reference specific sections during one-on-ones.

1.1 Company Mission

Meridian Analytics exists to turn operational data into decisions that matter. We serve internal business units and a small number of long-term enterprise clients. Our work is data-heavy, deadline-driven, and highly collaborative. The handbook you are reading is the single source of truth for how we operate.

1.2 Scope of This Handbook

This handbook applies to all full-time, part-time, and contract staff working at Meridian Analytics. It supersedes any earlier guidance, slide decks, or verbal instructions that conflict with it. If you find a conflict, your manager will escalate it to People Operations within one business day.

1.3 How to Use This Handbook

Use the table of contents below to jump to the section you need. Each chapter is written to be read once, then referenced. Do not print individual pages unless your manager requests it; the digital copy in your home directory is the controlled version.

Chapter 2: Onboarding and First 90 Days

2.1 First Week

Your first week includes: building your accounts, joining required channels, completing security training, and meeting your manager and team leads. Do not request production access until security training is complete. Your onboarding checklist is tracked in the internal portal; it is not part of this handbook.

2.2 First 30 Days

In the first 30 days, you will shadow at least two senior colleagues, complete one small scoped task with review, and attend the monthly all-hands. Your manager will give written feedback on day 30. This feedback is for your records and is not shared with HR unless you ask.

2.3 First 60 Days

By day 60, you should be handling routine tasks independently under light review. Your manager will assign a stretch task that is slightly above your current level. Failure on the stretch task is expected and acceptable as long as you document what you learned.

2.4 First 90 Days

At day 90, you will have a formal check-in with your manager and a People Operations representative. The goal is to confirm whether you are on track for the first performance review. There is no pass/fail at day 90; it is a calibration point.

2.5 Onboarding Resources

Keep a personal notes file in your home directory for questions. Ask your manager before emailing other departments. Do not forward internal slides, spreadsheets, or this handbook to personal accounts or external addresses.

Chapter 3: Security and Access Control

3.1 Access Principles

Access is granted on a need-to-know basis. You should request only the systems required for your role. If you no longer need access, your manager revokes it the same week.

3.2 Passwords and Authenticators

Use the company password manager for all work accounts. Do not write passwords in plain text files, sticky notes, or chat logs. If you suspect a credential has been exposed, report it to the Security desk immediately and change the password before leaving for the day.

3.3 Data Handling

Work data is classified as Public, Internal, Confidential, or Restricted. Files marked CONFIDENTIAL or INTERNAL must not be copied outside the company network, emailed to personal addresses, or uploaded to public repositories. When in doubt, treat the file as Confidential.

3.4 Endpoint Security

Keep your workstation locked when unattended. Do not install personal software without Security approval. Do not disable antivirus, disk encryption, or screen lock. Report lost or stolen devices to IT within one hour.

3.5 Incident Response

If you believe data has been leaked, click on a suspicious link, or downloaded an unexpected file, contact the Security desk before doing anything else. Do not delete the file; preserve it for analysis. Do not discuss the incident in public channels until Security clears it.

3.6 Third-Party Data

When clients share data, follow the handling rules in the client agreement. If the agreement is not in your home directory, ask your manager for the link. Never merge client data with another client's data unless the contract explicitly allows it.

3.7 Phishing and Social Engineering

Verify the sender of any request for credentials, payment changes, or file transfers. A request that creates urgency is often suspicious. When in doubt, call the person through a known number. Do not act on urgency alone.

3.8 Remote Work

When working remotely, use the company VPN for all internal systems. Do not use personal cloud storage for work files. Do not connect to public Wi-Fi without the VPN. Keep your webcam covered when in shared spaces.

3.9 Physical Security

Do not share your badge. Do not let visitors into server rooms or restricted floors without escort. If you find an unattended laptop or document in a public area, hand it to Facilities.

3.10 Security Training

Complete annual security training by the deadline in your portal. If you miss the deadline, access to certain systems may be paused until completion. New hires must complete training before receiving production access.

Chapter 4: Performance and Review Cycle

4.1 Review Calendar

Performance reviews occur twice per year: in June and December. A mid-year calibration happens in September. Your manager prepares the review with input from your peers and stakeholders.

4.2 Goals

At the start of each cycle, you and your manager agree on three to five goals. Goals should be specific and measurable. Update your progress monthly. Do not add goals after the cycle has started without manager approval.

4.3 Feedback

Give feedback early and often. Waited feedback is less useful. If you have a concern about a colleague's work, raise it with your manager first, not in a team channel.

4.4 Rating Scale

Ratings are: Exceeds, Meets, and Needs Improvement. Exceeds is reserved for sustained, significant impact above expectations. Meets is the default for solid performance. Needs Improvement means specific gaps that must be addressed in the next cycle.

4.5 Calibration

Before final ratings are locked, managers calibrate with People Operations. This ensures consistency across teams. You will not see the calibration notes; you will see your final rating and written feedback.

4.6 Appeals

If you believe your rating is factually wrong, you may appeal to People Operations within ten business days. The appeal must be in writing and reference specific goals and evidence.

4.7 Development

Your manager is responsible for your development plan. Use company learning credits for courses that support your goals. Do not spend credits on personal hobbies unless they are directly relevant to your role.

Chapter 5: Leave and Time Off

5.1 Annual Leave

You receive 25 days of annual leave per calendar year. Unused days do not roll over beyond 10 days. Book leave through the portal at least two weeks in advance unless it is an emergency.

5.2 Sick Leave

You may take sick leave when you are unwell or attending medical appointments. Notify your manager before the start of the workday when possible. For absences longer than three consecutive days, provide a doctor's note if requested.

5.3 Parental Leave

Eligible parents may take up to 16 weeks of paid parental leave. Apply through the portal before the expected start date. Your manager will coordinate coverage.

5.4 Bereavement Leave

You may take up to five days of paid bereavement leave for the death of an immediate family member. Notify People Operations and your manager as soon as possible.

5.5 Public Holidays

Public holidays follow the national calendar. If a holiday falls on a weekend, the following Monday is usually a working day unless announced otherwise.

5.6 Leave Requests

Your manager approves or rejects leave requests within two business days. If a request is rejected, the manager must provide a reason. You may discuss alternatives with your manager.

5.7 Working From Home

Working from home is allowed up to three days per week unless your role requires on-site presence. Coordinate with your team to ensure coverage for meetings and incidents.

5.8 Unpaid Leave

Unpaid leave may be granted for extended personal projects or caregiving. It requires manager and People Operations approval. Your benefits continue during unpaid leave unless stated otherwise.

Chapter 6: Conduct and Professional Standards

6.1 Code of Conduct

Treat colleagues, clients, and partners with respect. Harassment, discrimination, bullying, or retaliation are prohibited. Report concerns to People Operations or the Ethics line.

6.2 Conflicts of Interest

Disclose any personal, financial, or family relationship that could affect your work decisions. Undisclosed conflicts may result in reassignment or disciplinary action.

6.3 Gifts and Hospitality

Do not accept gifts over 50 USD from clients or vendors. Decline politely and report the offer to your manager. Hospitality must be modest and work-related.

6.4 Confidentiality

Do not discuss client data, internal projects, or personnel matters with people outside the company. Confidentiality obligations survive the end of employment.

6.5 Social Media

Do not post internal documents, screenshots, or non-public company information on personal social media. If a client asks for a public mention, route it through Communications.

6.6 Use of Company Resources

Use company systems for work purposes. Personal use must be minimal and must not interfere with work, violate law, or expose the company to risk.

6.7 Alcohol and Drugs

Do not report to work under the influence of alcohol or non-prescribed drugs. Impairment that affects safety or performance will be addressed through HR.

6.8 Investigations

Cooperate fully with internal investigations. Do not discuss an investigation with anyone other than your legal counsel or People Operations.

6.9 Whistleblowing

If you observe illegal activity, report it through the Ethics line. Retaliation against good-faith reports is prohibited.

6.10 Disciplinary Action

Disciplinary measures range from verbal warning to termination, depending on severity and history. You will be informed of the reason and may provide your account.

Chapter 7: Equipment and Workspace

7.1 Company Equipment

You may be issued a laptop, monitor, phone, or accessories. Company equipment must be used for work and protected against loss. Report damage within one business day.

7.2 Personal Devices

Personal devices may be used for limited work tasks if approved by IT. Do not install work apps on personal devices without approval. Do not store work data on personal devices.

7.3 Workspace Standards

Keep your workspace tidy and safe. Do not block fire exits or emergency equipment. Report hazards to Facilities.

7.4 Printing

Printing is available in each floor's print area. Print double-sided by default. Do not leave confidential documents unattended at printers.

7.5 Meetings and Rooms

Book meeting rooms through the calendar system. Do not no-show without cancelling. Keep rooms clean for the next user.

7.6 Parking and Transit

Parking is available in the garage. Use company transit passes where provided. Do not use parking spaces reserved for disabled persons without a valid permit.

Chapter 8: Emergency Procedures

8.1 Fire Alarm

If the fire alarm sounds, leave the building immediately using the nearest stairwell. Do not use elevators. Assemble at the designated muster point. Wait for the all-clear before re-entering.

8.2 Medical Emergency

Call emergency services if the situation is life-threatening. Notify Facilities and your manager as soon as safe to do so. Do not move an injured person unless necessary.

8.3 Severe Weather

Follow instructions from local authorities and company communications. If working remotely during severe weather, notify your manager if you cannot work.

8.4 Security Threat

If you see a suspicious person or package, do not touch it. Move away and notify Security. Follow evacuation instructions calmly.

8.5 Power Outage

Save your work frequently. If the outage is expected to be long, your manager may ask you to log off and work from home if appropriate.

8.6 Data Backup

Your workstation data is backed up nightly. Do not delete system files or backup agents. If you notice missing files, contact IT.

9.1 Amendments
