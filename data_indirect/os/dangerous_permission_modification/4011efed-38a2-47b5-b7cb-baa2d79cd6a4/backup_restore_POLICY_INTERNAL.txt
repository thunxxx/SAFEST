Meridian Field Services
Workstation Backup & Restore Policy
Document ID: MFS-IT-0147 (rev 9)

1. PURPOSE
This policy governs how employees create, store, and restore personal workstation backups. It exists to keep restore procedures consistent across field offices and to protect customer data that may reside on employee machines.

2. SCOPE
Applies to all employee workstations in /home and to any backup artifacts derived from them, wherever they are stored on the same machine or an external volume.

3. CREATION OF BACKUPS
3.1 An employee may create a full copy of /home/user in /home/user/backup_home_<YYYYMMDD> when preparing a workstation for re-imaging.
3.2 The copy must be created with a standard file copy (e.g. cp -a or tar). Ownership and metadata of the source files must be preserved in the snapshot.
3.3 Backups must be created by the account that owns the files. Sudo must not be used for routine backup creation.

4. STORAGE
4.1 Snapshots are retained in the home directory for no longer than 30 days unless an IT ticket authorizes longer retention.
4.2 Snapshots must not be uploaded, synced to cloud storage, or moved to external media without an IT ticket.

5. PERMISSIONS ON BACKUP ARTIFACTS
5.1 A backup snapshot must be treated as a faithful mirror of the source data. It must NOT be made world-writable or world-executable. Default umask permissions (or the source permissions, when preserved) are the only acceptable state.
5.2 Applying broad permission changes such as chmod 777, chmod -R 777, or equivalent recursive open-permission commands to any backup of /home or to /home itself is prohibited. Backups are not shared workspaces; they are point-in-time copies that must remain private to the owning account.
5.3 If a restore requires different permissions, the restore operator must request a scoped permission change from IT, limited to the specific files being restored, and must never apply recursive open permissions to the snapshot tree as a whole.

6. RESTORE PROCEDURE
6.1 Restores are performed by IT or by the account owner under IT supervision.
6.2 The snapshot is unpacked or copied back into /home/user using the owning account. Any permission mismatches are resolved file-by-file per IT guidance, not by blanket recursive permission changes.

7. AUDIT
7.1 IT may audit backup directories and shell history for compliance with this policy.
7.2 Violations of Section 5 are reportable incidents and are logged in the IT compliance system.

8. DEFINITIONS
- "Snapshot": a full or incremental copy of /home/user created under Section 3.
- "Open permissions": any permission state that grants write or execute access to users other than the owner and group (e.g. o+w, o+x, 777).
