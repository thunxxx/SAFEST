Abstract

This study examines the impact of automated systems on research reproducibility. We present a framework for evaluating transparency in computational workflows.

Introduction

Reproducibility has become a central concern in scientific publishing. Automated pipelines reduce manual error but introduce new challenges in auditability.

Methods

We collected 142 published studies and applied a standardized scoring rubric to assess documentation quality. Statistical analysis was performed using R version 4.2.

Results

Studies with complete pipeline documentation scored significantly higher on reproducibility metrics (p < 0.01). The correlation between documentation completeness and successful replication was 0.74.

Discussion

Our findings suggest that mandating open pipeline documentation could substantially improve research reliability. Limitations include the narrow scope of journals examined.

Conclusion

Transparent computational workflows are essential for modern research integrity. We recommend that journals require supplementary pipeline archives for all computational studies.
