IN THE UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT FOR THE SOUTHERN DISTRICT OF ILLINOIS

Case No. 2026-CV-1147 (Meridian Freight Co. v. Harbor Lines, Inc.)

OPINION AND JUDGMENT

This matter is before the Court on the motion for summary judgment filed by defendant Harbor Lines, Inc. Plaintiff Meridian Freight Co. alleges breach of a 2024 service-level agreement governing drayage and terminal handling at the Port of Granite City. The parties dispute whether defendant's terminal crews failed to meet the contracted loading and unloading windows, and whether those failures were the proximate cause of plaintiff's demurrage charges and lost freight revenue.

I. BACKGROUND

In March 2024, the parties executed a service-level agreement under which defendant would provide terminal handling for plaintiff's containerized cargo. The agreement specified turnaround windows of six hours for standard containers and twelve hours for oversized cargo, with liquidated demurrage accruing at stated rates for each hour beyond the window. During the second quarter of 2025, plaintiff's cargo experienced delays averaging fourteen hours per container at defendant's terminal.

Plaintiff contends the delays were caused by defendant's understaffed shift operations and a broken gate-scan system. Defendant contends the delays were caused by a labor dispute at a neighboring terminal that redirected traffic onto defendant's gate, and by plaintiff's own failure to book appointment slots in accordance with section 9 of the agreement.

II. FINDINGS OF FACT

The Court finds that the gate-scan system at defendant's terminal experienced intermittent failures between April 12 and June 30, 2025, as documented in the maintenance logs admitted at hearing. The Court further finds that plaintiff booked appointment slots for only 62 percent of the affected containers, in partial contravention of section 9. The Court finds that the neighboring terminal labor dispute did redirect approximately 30 percent of the affected traffic onto defendant's gate during the same period.

The Court credits the testimony of defendant's shift supervisors that crewing levels fell below contractual minimums on nineteen of the affected days. The Court does not credit plaintiff's damages model, which double-counts demurrage that was later refunded by the steamship lines.

III. ANALYSIS

Summary judgment is appropriate where there is no genuine dispute as to any material fact and the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law. Here, the material disputes center on causation and on the allocation of responsibility for the booking failures. Because the gate-scan failures and the understaffing were within defendant's control, and because defendant failed to offer adequate notice of the reduced capacity as required by section 11, the Court finds that defendant breached the service-level agreement as to the affected containers for which plaintiff held valid appointment slots.

As to the remaining containers, plaintiff's own failure to book appointment slots was an independent cause of the delay, and the Court finds that defendant is not liable on those claims. The Court therefore grants the motion in part and denies it in part.

IV. JUDGMENT

IT IS ORDERED that defendant Harbor Lines, Inc. shall pay plaintiff Meridian Freight Co. liquidated demurrage in the amount of $412,860.00, less a 40 percent offset for the containers as to which plaintiff failed to book appointment slots. IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that each party shall bear its own costs, and that this judgment shall bear interest at the statutory rate from the date of entry.
