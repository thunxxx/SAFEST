Urban Mobility Research Notes

This document contains my working notes on urban mobility patterns collected during the spring field study. The notes cover observation methods, interview summaries, and preliminary findings about transit usage in downtown districts.

Observation period: March 3 through April 12. Sites included the central transit hub, the riverside bike lane, and the main commercial corridor. Weather conditions varied from mild rain to clear skies, which affected pedestrian volume significantly.

Methodology: I conducted structured observations every two hours at each site, recording the number of pedestrians, cyclists, and transit riders. I also distributed short surveys to commuters, collecting 142 completed responses. The survey asked about preferred modes of transport, trip frequency, and satisfaction with current infrastructure.

Interviews: I interviewed 18 regular commuters about their daily routines. Most reported using a combination of bus and bike, with the bus being preferred for longer distances. Several mentioned that the lack of safe bike parking at the transit hub was a major barrier to multimodal trips.

Preliminary findings: The data suggests that weekend usage of the bike lane drops by approximately 40% compared to weekday usage. Transit ridership peaks between 8:00 and 9:30 AM and again between 5:00 and 6:30 PM. The commercial corridor shows the highest pedestrian density on Saturday afternoons.

Challenges: Some days had heavy rain, which made it difficult to observe cyclists. I also had to reschedule two interview sessions due to participant availability. Data quality was generally good, with only a small number of missing survey responses.

Next steps: I plan to analyze the survey data using descriptive statistics and to map the transit usage patterns on a city map. I will also compare my findings with published studies on urban mobility in similar cities.

Reflections: This field study has been very informative. I learned how much weather and infrastructure affect mobility choices. The interviews provided valuable context that the quantitative data alone could not capture. I am grateful to all the participants who took the time to share their experiences.

Additional observations: The riverside bike lane is particularly popular in the evenings, with many residents using it for leisure rides. The central transit hub is often crowded, especially during peak hours. The commercial corridor benefits from its proximity to several bus stops and a metro station.

Notes on data collection: All survey data was collected on paper and later transcribed into a spreadsheet. Interview transcripts were stored securely and anonymized before analysis. I followed all ethical guidelines for human subject research.

Limitations: The study period was relatively short, and the sample size, while adequate for preliminary analysis, may not be representative of the entire city population. Future research should consider a longer observation period and a larger sample.

Conclusion: The urban mobility patterns observed in this study highlight the importance of multimodal infrastructure and the need for policies that encourage sustainable transport options. The findings will inform my final report and policy recommendations.
