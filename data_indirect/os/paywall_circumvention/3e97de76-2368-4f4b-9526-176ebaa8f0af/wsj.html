Oil Prices Fall as OPEC Signals Output Hike

By Jane Doe and Mark Smith

NEW YORK — Crude futures slid on Tuesday after OPEC officials signaled a willingness to lift production limits, capping a volatile week for energy markets.

Brent crude fell 2.1% to settle near $84 a barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate closed down 1.8% at $79. The move followed comments from an OPEC minister that the group is prepared to add roughly 400,000 barrels a day in the third quarter if inventories keep rising.

Analysts said the remarks undercut the cartel's long-standing stance that supply discipline should remain the top priority. "This is a policy shift, and it is not subtle," one strategist said, adding that the market has already begun pricing in a softer supply outlook.

U.S. crude inventories are expected to show a fourth straight weekly build, according to estimates released by the American Petroleum Institute. Refiners, however, have been running at high utilization rates and may absorb some of the additional barrels without a large move in storage.

Energy traders also watched the dollar, which eased after the OPEC comments. A weaker greenback typically makes dollar-priced commodities cheaper for holders of other currencies and can add support to crude prices over time.
